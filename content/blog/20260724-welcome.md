To those who know me, this goes without saying, but I am _quite the nerd_.
There have been numerous occasions where I've apparently provided amusement to
those around me through the sheer gap between their definition of what
constitutes "relaxing activity", and my own. Apparently things like reading a
[physics textbook](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/), over-engineering
a coding text editor setup (go [neovim](https://neovim.io/)!) or playing a
series of rapid [chess games](https://lichess.org/) for an unhealthy amount of
time don't make the cut for "relaxing activities" for the majority of people,
but I suppose we can agree to disagree.

Recently, I've been itching to make my learning process a bit more active
through the process of writing. I hear that the process of teaching drastically
improves one's long-term retention and comprehension[^1][^2][^3][^4], and that
active learning is a good thing in general. Unfortunately, it's not possible
for me to always find someone willing to be taught by me about my most recent
obsession (although my girlfriend is too nice to admit it, I think she can
sometimes find my impromptu ramblings about [information
theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07867v3) a little bit boring), but perhaps
the next best thing would be to write the occasional mini-essay about a topic
which I find interesting, and want to consolidate in my own head. That's why I
decided to start a blog. If I am going to be writing mini-essays about cool
stuff, then why not put it on my website, instead of filing them into some deep
dark folder that I will inevitably misplace and forget about? With any luck,
the blog posts might eventually serve as reference material for either my
future self - who's probably forgotten all about the topic - or even cooler,
someone else out there who stumbles upon them!

There is one other important reason why I thought it would be worth starting a
blog. As a researcher and scientist, a significant portion of my future work is
probably (hopefully!) going to involve writing text. More specifically,
academic, scientific, expository text. And to be honest, I still struggle with
that kind of writing to this day. For some reason, when it comes to writing
manuscripts, I get a bad case of writer's block. And I think it's a very
particular, academic flavour of writer's block that I know is also experienced
by some of my research colleagues as well as myself. It's the sort of
perfectionist kind, where you say: "Ah, I have already explained X in this
paragraph already in a completely reasonable manner, but maybe if I spend an
extra 5 minutes, I can make the sentences sound just slightly better," or
"Something's off with figure 1A... Maybe the x-axis tick labels need to be of
font size (_insert ridiculously specific font size_) instead of (_insert a
similar, also ridiculously specific font size_)." Do these perfectionist
compulsions eventually help make the final manuscript product better? I
suppose, to some extent. But at a certain point there are diminishing returns,
and it just isn't worth the time. No manuscript is ever perfect, and a good,
published manuscript is [almost always
better](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jkAw87ZIwQA) than a slightly better one
that is stuck in a perfectionist writer hell loop _ad infinitum_. For me, this
personal blog, where I can write some low-stakes mini-essays, still on academic
topics, is a way to practice just writing stuff. To enjoy the process of
writing, and to be happy publishing something without giving into the urge of
making it _perfect_.

I want to conclude my first and already ramble-y post by saying that my blog
posts here will always be 100% human generated (at the time of writing, it
still feels incredible, both exciting and terrifying, that AI-generated writing
is practically indistinguishable from human writing...). Like I've explained
above, the reason why I'm starting the blog is for me to write little
mini-essays, so generating the content using a machine would defeat the
purpose. To some, whether the text is my own or proxied through an AI assistant
may not matter very much, but for those other people, I thought it worth
mentioning. And to any such readers out there, I hope that you enjoy the
personal touch that comes from the messy, imperfect and human entropy endowed
to the content here by my brain!
